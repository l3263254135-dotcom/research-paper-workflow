# Research Positioning

Use before analysis or drafting. Read the supplied literature and project records; distinguish `BOOK-SUPPORTED` and `ARTICLE-SUPPORTED` workflow advice from local scientific evidence.

## Produce

- object, treatment/exposure, response, comparison, spatial and temporal scale;
- one primary hypothesis, at least one alternative, and observable predictions for each;
- verified knowledge gap, contribution, intended readers, and provisional title;
- sprint/match/safe journal list with official source URLs and query dates;
- reasons a candidate is unsuitable and explicit transfer conditions.
- minimum publishable unit decision with reasons for combining or splitting results;
- title candidates that are precise, searchable, concise, and no stronger than the evidence;
- reader map covering specialist readers, adjacent readers, and practical users.

## Constraints

Do not claim novelty, priority, complete mechanism, or causal effect without a verified basis. Separate a phenomenon paper, process-constraint paper, attribution paper, and prediction paper. If the evidence cannot discriminate hypotheses, say so and narrow the contribution.

## Acceptance

The question can be supported or falsified by available data; every contribution sentence maps to a source or a clearly labelled inference; unresolved design choices are listed rather than silently assumed; the journal choice is based on audience, scope, article type, evidence threshold, format, and access rather than prestige alone.
